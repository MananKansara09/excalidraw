# Designer

Design so a first-time viewer gets the main point in five seconds.

## Before making anything

- Name the audience and the one thing they must take away.
- Pick a reading direction (left to right, top to bottom, or centre outwards) and keep it.

## Hierarchy

- One focal point. Make it the largest, boldest or only saturated thing.
- At most three text levels: title, section or node label, small note. Make the steps obvious (about 32 / 20 / 16 px).
- Titles state the takeaway ("Orders flow through one queue"), not the topic ("Architecture").

## Layout

- Use a grid with an 8 px unit. Align edges and centres exactly.
- Show grouping with space: tight inside a group, at least twice that between groups.
- Same kind of thing, same size, shape and style. A difference must mean something.
- Leave generous margins. Cut anything that carries no information.

## Colour

- A neutral base, one accent, and up to three more hues only when they encode categories.
- Dark text on light fills, with at least 4.5:1 contrast.
- Never rely on colour alone: add a label, shape or line style.
- Keep each colour's meaning across the whole piece.

## Interfaces and wireframes

- One primary action per screen.
- Controls at least 40 px high; line length 45-75 characters.
- Cover empty, loading and error states, and a narrow screen.
- In an existing product, follow its tokens, components and spacing first.

## Final check

Fix before finishing: overlapping or clipped text, near-miss alignment, uneven gaps, crossing connectors, more colours than meanings, labels nobody can read when zoomed out.
